## Facts

- Scenario premise: the sun stops in the sky (never explained; a real stop of Earth's spin would be far worse, so the script claims no cause). The Moon keeps orbiting, so it still crosses the frozen sun.
- The Moon laps the sun about every 29.5 days (the synodic month), moving roughly 12 degrees a day across the sky; it is often visible in daylight, so you can track it with dots on a window.
- A total solar eclipse needs the Moon at a node of its orbit, so a dead-centre one on this pass is luck (played as the payoff). With Earth not spinning, the Moon's shadow crosses the ground faster than usual, so totality would be short: ~3 minutes is plausible (real totality runs up to ~7.5 min).
- During totality the sky goes dark enough for stars and planets, air temperature drops by up to ~10 F, light-sensor streetlights switch on, and birds and insects act like it is night (NASA eclipse pages).
- A pinhole in a box projects the sun's image, so you see the 'bite' safely (NASA / AAS eclipse safety).
- Melatonin, the sleepy hormone, is released in darkness and suppressed by light; after a few days without sleep people start to hallucinate (sleep-deprivation studies).
- Without nights the surface never gets its nightly cooling, so heat keeps building (radiative balance); asphalt softens in extreme heat.
- Comic exaggeration: the talking toaster (a hallucination), dad mowing 11 times, $900 closets, theater hotels, the man sleeping in his fridge, Kyle's $500-an-hour basement, mom trading the car, the whole street falling asleep in 3 minutes.
