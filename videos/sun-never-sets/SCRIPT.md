## Facts

- Scenario premise: the sun just stops in the sky (never explained; a real stop of Earth's spin would be far worse, so the script does not claim a cause).
- Melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy, is released by the pineal gland in darkness; light suppresses it (NIH / Sleep Foundation).
- At night Earth's surface loses heat by radiating infrared to space; without nights the daily cooling disappears and heat keeps building (basic radiative balance; why deserts cool fast at night).
- A few feet underground the soil barely feels daily swings; about 10-20 ft down it sits near the local yearly average, roughly 50-60 F over much of the US (the basis of geothermal heat pumps and root cellars). Heat takes weeks to months to soak down.
- Heat waves send air-conditioning demand past what grids can supply, causing blackouts (e.g. California rolling blackouts, August 2020).
- Asphalt softens in extreme heat; people in midnight-sun places (Alaska, northern Norway) cover windows with tinfoil or blackout blinds to sleep.
- Comic exaggeration: 120 F on the porch after 40 days, owls on strike, the pool turning to soup, six breakfasts a day, the egg on the car, the flamingo plug.
