## Facts

- Without the moon only the sun raises tides; its tidal pull is ~46% of the moon's, leaving roughly a third of today's tidal range (ScienceABC, Astronoo; ZME Science says 50-75% lower). Script: 'stops a third of the way'.
- No giant wave: the moon's tidal bulge would settle like an ordinary ebb over hours, not surge (Live Science / Space.com 'what if the moon disappeared'; forum physics agrees). The bedsheet analogy is ours.
- The sea pulling back from the shore is a real tsunami warning sign (Brad's line is a true fact misapplied).
- Full moon ~0.25 lux vs clear moonless night with starlight + airglow ~0.002 lux: ~125x (Wikipedia 'Daylight' / 'Orders of magnitude (illuminance)'). Script: 'a hundred times darker' (vs a full-moon night).
- Moonlight washes out faint stars; dark moonless skies show far more stars and the Milky Way (real). 'Exploding with stars' is comic exaggeration.
- Light traps catch more insects on dark (new-moon) nights; 'every moth for fifty miles finds Brad' is comic exaggeration.
- 4,000 people, 500 sandbags, 50 miles: story numbers, not fact claims.
